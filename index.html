The fold — 2 creases, 4 layers, 8 faces

A square, folded in half and in half again. Every face keeps a fixed printed side and a fixed reverse side; creasing never swaps them.

printed — 1f 2f 3f 4f reverse — 1b 2b 3b 4b
  1. Left half onto right half. 2 layers, right-hand rectangle.
  2. Top half onto bottom half. 4 layers, one quarter of the square.
  3. Hover. Nothing touches anything, so a wiped face stays wiped. The only transfer left is face against face — and this fold never buries a face you have already used.

The honest bit

Hovering is not a detail, it is the mechanism. Set the packet down and the face you already wiped picks up whatever was on that surface — switch the toggle above and watch step 2 go red.

You must turn the packet over once, while it is still 4 layers thick, to reach the single face on its underside. That one flip, pinched along a crease in mid-air, is the tightest moment in the whole procedure.

And it is wasteful on purpose. A 30 cm square gives 225 cm² per face against a 32 cm² cheek. You are buying a guarantee, not efficiency — a 15 cm pocket square covers a whole face in eight never-reused strokes.
